The result is out, you have crossed the pass mark, and the question every foreign medical graduate eventually faces is suddenly very real: **what after FMGE**? The screening test was only the gate. On the other side of it lies a clear, regulated sequence — provisional registration, a year of internship in India, and then permanent registration — after which the whole landscape of Indian medical practice, including NEET PG, opens up to you. This guide walks through each step in order, so you always know what comes next and what paperwork it needs.

## What After FMGE: The Roadmap at a Glance

The path from FMGE result to independent practice has four stages:

- **Provisional registration** with a State Medical Council, which allows you to begin internship.
- **The 12-month CRMI** (Compulsory Rotating Medical Internship) at an approved hospital in India.
- **Permanent registration** with a State Medical Council once the internship is complete.
- **Your next move** — NEET PG preparation, a medical officer post, or another career direction.

None of these steps is optional, and they must happen in this order. You cannot start the internship without provisional registration, and you cannot get permanent registration without completing the internship. Treat the sequence as a pipeline: while one stage is running, prepare the documents for the next.

## Step 1: Provisional Registration

Your first task after the result is to apply for **provisional registration** with the State Medical Council of the state where you intend to do your internship. This registration is what legally permits you to train as an intern in an Indian hospital.

Applications typically require your FMGE result, your primary medical qualification (the degree from your university abroad), your passport and visa pages covering your study period, and your Class 10 and 12 certificates. Councils differ in their exact checklists, fees and processing times, and many now accept applications online — so read your chosen council's current instructions carefully before you apply. Apply early: internship seats at popular hospitals fill on schedules you do not control, and a delayed registration can cost you a full allotment cycle.

One practical point foreign graduates often miss: your degree documents may need attestation or verification through your university or embassy channels. Start that process the week your result arrives, not the week you hope to join.

## Step 2: The 12-Month CRMI Internship

The **CRMI** is a structured, rotating internship across the major clinical departments — medicine, surgery, obstetrics and gynaecology, paediatrics, orthopaedics, emergency care, community medicine and the allied branches — at a hospital approved for the purpose. It exists for a specific reason: your degree was earned in a different health system, and this year grounds you in Indian clinical practice, Indian patient expectations and Indian hospital workflows.

Take this year seriously as training, not as a formality:

- **Show up like a doctor, not a student.** Interns who take ownership of patients learn procedures, documentation and clinical judgement far faster.
- **Learn the system.** Referral pathways, medico-legal basics, government health programmes and record-keeping are skills you will use for your entire career in India.
- **Protect your basics.** Keep revising clinical concepts alongside duty hours — it pays off directly if NEET PG is your next step.
- **Get your completion certificate in order.** Your internship completion documentation is the key that unlocks permanent registration; chase signatures and rotations paperwork as you go, not at the end.

Under the current **NMC framework**, foreign medical graduates complete this internship in India as a condition for permanent registration. Rules around internship — including where it may be done and how prior internship abroad is treated — have been clarified and adjusted by official notices over time, so verify the current position on the NMC website before making commitments.

## Step 3: Permanent Registration with a State Medical Council

With your internship completion certificate in hand, you apply for **permanent registration** with a State Medical Council. This is the registration that makes you a fully registered medical practitioner in India — eligible to practise independently, to apply for government medical officer posts, and to appear for NEET PG as a registered doctor.

You generally register in one state, and if you later move, registration can be transferred or additionally recognised as per the applicable rules. Keep your registration certificate, and every document behind it, organised for life: employers, credential verification agencies and foreign regulators will ask for this paper trail again and again.

## Step 4: NEET PG and Beyond

For most graduates, the next big decision is **NEET PG** — the national entrance examination for MD, MS and PG diploma seats, conducted by NBEMS. Some doctors prepare during the internship year; others take dedicated time after it. Both approaches work; what matters is an honest assessment of your baseline and a plan you can sustain. Our [resources](/resources/) section and the planning guides there can help you think through timelines.

Beyond PG, the registered doctor's map is wide: government service through UPSC CMS and state commissions, hospital practice, academics, research and more. The FMGE was the narrow gate; what lies after it is the full profession.

## Mistakes to Avoid in the First Year

- **Waiting for "complete information" before applying.** Councils publish checklists; apply with what they ask, when they ask.
- **Choosing an internship hospital only by convenience.** Training quality in this year shapes your clinical confidence for years.
- **Letting documents scatter.** Keep one folder — physical and scanned — with every certificate, in order.
- **Treating the internship year as dead time for PG.** Even light, consistent revision compounds.

Wherever you are in this sequence, steady daily practice keeps your clinical knowledge exam-ready. Q: Can I start my internship immediately after the FMGE result?
A: You can begin once you hold provisional registration from a State Medical Council and are allotted a seat at an approved hospital. Apply for provisional registration as soon as your result is declared.
Q: Is the 12-month internship compulsory for foreign medical graduates?
A: Yes. Under the NMC framework, completing the 12-month CRMI in India is required before a foreign medical graduate can obtain permanent registration. Check the NMC website for the current official notices.
Q: Can I appear for NEET PG after FMGE?
A: Yes, once you meet the eligibility conditions, which include completing your internship and holding registration as required by the current NEET PG information bulletin from NBEMS. Always verify the current bulletin.
Q: What is the difference between provisional and permanent registration?
A: Provisional registration allows you to undergo internship training. Permanent registration, granted after internship completion, makes you a fully registered medical practitioner eligible for independent practice and government posts.
