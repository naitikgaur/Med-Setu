For foreign medical graduates, the **internship after fmge** is the final bridge between a degree earned abroad and a licence to practise in India — and it is the step students research least. Many reach it unsure whether they apply, who allots the hospital, whether they are paid, or what happens if paperwork is delayed. This article explains the CRMI year clearly: what it is, how it is allotted, what the work involves, and how it ends in permanent registration. Rules are framed by the NMC and administered through states, so always confirm the current official notices for your state and session.

## What CRMI is, and where it fits in your journey

**CRMI** stands for **Compulsory Rotating Medical Internship** — the structured, supervised clinical internship that every doctor must complete before permanent registration in India. For a foreign medical graduate, the sequence is:

1. Complete your MBBS abroad, including the internship required at the foreign institution under the FMGL framework
2. Clear the **FMGE** in India
3. Obtain provisional registration through the state medical council process
4. Complete **12 months of CRMI** in India at an approved institution
5. Apply for **permanent registration** with the state medical council

Only after step 5 are you a fully registered medical practitioner in India. Notice that the CRMI year is not a repetition of your foreign internship: the foreign internship is part of your degree's eligibility conditions, while CRMI is India's own supervised practice requirement, and the current framework requires foreign graduates to complete it in India. Skipping or shortcutting it is not an option, so plan for the year from the beginning — financially and personally.

## Internship after FMGE: how the allotment works

The internship is not arranged by walking into a hospital of your choice. In general terms, allotment happens through the **state machinery**: after clearing the FMGE and completing the provisional registration formalities, candidates are allotted internship seats in approved hospitals through the process run by the state medical council or the designated state authority, which may involve counselling rounds, merit or application-based allocation, and document verification.

The practical details — application windows, required documents, how preferences are taken, and which hospitals have seats — are published by the respective state authorities for each cycle, and they differ between states. Two habits protect you here:

- **Follow your state medical council's official notices** from the month you expect your FMGE result, not after it
- **Keep your document file complete and ready**: degree, transcripts, eligibility and internship documents from abroad, FMGE result, and identity records, with copies organised in advance

Delays in this phase are most often paperwork delays, and they are almost always avoidable with early organisation.

## What the CRMI year actually involves

CRMI is a **rotating** internship: you move through the major clinical departments — typically including medicine, surgery, obstetrics and gynaecology, paediatrics, community medicine, orthopaedics, emergency and casualty, and allied postings — spending defined periods in each, under supervision, in an Indian hospital setting.

Treat the year as what it is: your apprenticeship in Indian practice. You will take histories and present cases, assist in procedures and deliveries, learn the documentation and referral systems, work night duties, and absorb how Indian hospitals actually function — patient volumes, resource realities, communication with families, and teamwork with nursing and senior staff. Foreign graduates often find this year quietly transformative: it converts examination knowledge into bedside confidence inside the system where they will spend their careers.

Your attitude in postings matters. Consultants remember interns who show up prepared, follow up their patients and ask precise questions. The relationships and reputation you build in CRMI frequently shape your first job opportunities after registration.

## The stipend question, answered honestly

Students frequently ask what the CRMI stipend pays. The honest answer is that **stipend arrangements vary by state and institution**, and are governed by the applicable state rules for interns at the time. Some interns receive a stipend in line with what the state's interns are paid; arrangements have historically differed across states and have been the subject of representation by foreign graduates.

So plan conservatively: budget for the internship year as a year of living costs, treat any stipend as support rather than salary, and check the current official position in your state when your allotment approaches. ## After CRMI: permanent registration and what comes next

On completing CRMI, you apply to the state medical council for **permanent registration**, submitting your internship completion certificate along with the other prescribed documents. With permanent registration in hand, you can practise independently in India, apply for government and private positions, and plan further steps such as postgraduate entrance preparation.

A few closing pieces of advice for the whole final phase:

- **Protect your documents** — every delay in this phase traces back to a missing or mismatched paper
- **Choose learning over comfort in postings** — the skills of this year carry into residency and practice
- **Stay registered and compliant** — follow council communications and renewals as your career begins
