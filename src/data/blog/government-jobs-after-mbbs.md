For many doctors, the most attractive first job is a secure, structured one — and **government jobs after mbbs** offer exactly that: a defined role, a service career, and the chance to practise medicine where it is needed most. The routes are several, and they are often confused with each other. UPSC's Combined Medical Services, state health services, the Railways, the Defence medical services and public-sector undertakings each recruit doctors differently. This guide separates the routes clearly, so you can target the one that fits your plans.

## Government Jobs After MBBS: The Main Routes

- **UPSC Combined Medical Services (CMS)** — the central route into services such as the Railways medical service, central health services and municipal health services of Delhi.
- **State health services** — medical officer posts recruited through state public service commissions and state health departments.
- **Defence medical services** — the Armed Forces Medical Services, including entries linked to AFMC and direct recruitment for the Army Medical Corps and its sister services.
- **Railways** — doctors serve in railway hospitals and health units, largely through the CMS route and specific railway recruitments.
- **PSUs and other central bodies** — organisations such as Coal India, ONGC and municipal corporations recruit doctors for their hospitals and occupational health services through their own notifications.

Each route has its own notification, eligibility window and selection method. The common thread: you must be a registered medical practitioner, and most routes open only after internship completion.

## UPSC CMS: The Central Gateway

The **Combined Medical Services examination**, conducted by the UPSC, is the best-known central entry for MBBS doctors. It recruits for several services in one examination — historically including the medical services of the Indian Railways, the Central Health Service, and the health services of the Municipal Corporation of Delhi, among others. Selection combines a computer-based written examination with an interview/personality test, as per the UPSC's official notification for that year.

CMS deserves a place in many doctors' plans because it is a single, predictable, annually-conducted gateway with a syllabus rooted in the MBBS curriculum — the same clinical knowledge base as NEET PG preparation, applied to a different paper. If you are preparing seriously for one, you are building most of the foundation for the other. Always read the current UPSC notification for services included, eligibility and the exam scheme, since details are notified afresh each cycle.

## State Services and Medical Officer Posts

Every state runs hospitals and primary health centres staffed by **medical officers** recruited through its public service commission or health department. These posts are the clinical backbone of public healthcare — outpatient care, emergency duties, national programme implementation and medico-legal work at district and block level. Recruitment patterns vary by state: some conduct written examinations, others recruit on merit and interview, and many run periodic drives when vacancies are sanctioned.

For a young doctor, a state medical officer post offers immediate, broad clinical responsibility — you will see more varied medicine in a district posting than in almost any other first job. Our companion guide on medical officer examinations explains the state PSC route in detail.

## Railways, Defence and PSU Medicine

**Railway medicine** runs a nationwide network of hospitals and health units caring for railway employees and their families. Entry is principally through UPSC CMS and through specific railway recruitment notifications, including contract engagements announced from time to time.

**Defence medical services** offer a uniformed career: doctors serve with the Army, Navy and Air Force medical services, combining military life with hospital and field medicine. Entry routes include those connected with **AFMC** and direct entries for civilian medical graduates, governed by the services' own notifications and medical standards. It is a distinctive life — postings across the country, service discipline, and medicine practised in settings no civilian job replicates. Read the official recruitment notices carefully; eligibility, age and service conditions are specific and change with each notification.

**PSUs and autonomous bodies** — coal, petroleum, steel and other public-sector organisations, plus institutions like ESI hospitals — recruit doctors for their own facilities through individual advertisements. These are scattered opportunities rather than a single exam; tracking the organisations that interest you is the strategy.

## How to Prepare and Apply Smartly

- **Keep your foundation exam-ready.** CMS and most state examinations draw on the MBBS curriculum; consistent question practice is the core preparation for all of them.
- **Track notifications officially.** UPSC, state PSC and service headquarters websites are the only reliable sources — set a monthly routine of checking them.
- **Keep documents permanently ready.** Registration certificate, internship completion, degree and category certificates — government recruitment windows are short, and paperwork delays disqualify more candidates than tough papers do.
- **Decide your priority order in advance.** Central services, your home state, a uniformed career — each implies different postings and lifestyles. Know your preference before results force a quick choice.

A word about how these careers actually unfold, because entry is only the first chapter. Government medical services are cadre careers: you join at an entry grade and progress through defined seniority and promotion structures, with postings that can range from primary health centres to large teaching institutions over a career. Transfers and rural postings are a normal part of service life, especially early on, and many doctors find the breadth of those early postings — running a facility, handling emergencies with limited resources, implementing national programmes — to be the most formative experience of their professional lives. Government service also combines with specialisation in structured ways: service doctors may become eligible for postgraduate opportunities under service quotas or sponsorship arrangements as per the rules of their cadre and the current admission regulations. If that possibility matters to you, read the service rules and the current NEET PG bulletin carefully rather than relying on hearsay — the details differ by service and change over time.

Q: What is the UPSC CMS exam for doctors?
A: The Combined Medical Services (CMS) examination, conducted by UPSC, recruits MBBS doctors into central services such as the Railways medical service, Central Health Service and Delhi municipal health services, through a written examination and interview as notified each year.
Q: Can I apply for government medical jobs immediately after MBBS?
A: Most government medical posts require completion of internship and registration with a State Medical Council. Some recruitments open applications to final-stage candidates as per their notification — always check the official notice.
Q: How do doctors join the Defence medical services?
A: Through routes notified by the Armed Forces Medical Services, including entries connected with AFMC and direct recruitment for civilian medical graduates. Eligibility, medical standards and service terms are specified in each official notification.
Q: Are state medical officer jobs filled through an exam?
A: It varies by state. State public service commissions and health departments recruit medical officers through written examinations, merit-and-interview processes, or periodic recruitment drives, as announced in official notifications.
