Every year, students discover too late that their foreign medical college does not meet India's licensing conditions, and that discovery can cost them their eligibility to practise medicine in India. Learning **how to check nmc approved colleges** properly — using primary sources, not agent promises — is the single most important step in the whole MBBS abroad journey. The good news is that the check is not difficult. It takes a few hours of careful work, and it can protect six years of your life and your family's savings.

## First, understand what "NMC approved" actually means

The phrase needs unpacking, because it misleads many families. The **National Medical Commission (NMC)** does not publish a simple list of approved foreign colleges the way it lists Indian medical colleges. Instead, the NMC's **Foreign Medical Graduate Licentiate (FMGL) Regulations 2021** set the conditions a foreign medical qualification must satisfy for its holder to be eligible for licensing in India. A college is "safe" when studying there lets you meet every one of those conditions.

The key FMGL conditions to verify are:

- The medical course lasts **at least 54 months**
- The medium of instruction is **English**
- The programme includes proper **clinical training** in teaching hospitals
- You complete a **12-month internship at the same foreign institution** after the course
- The curriculum covers the subjects required for licensing in India

After returning, you must still clear the **FMGE** and complete a 12-month **CRMI internship in India** before permanent registration. So when you check a college, you are really checking one question: *will this degree keep me eligible at every stage?* Always confirm the current wording of the regulations on the official NMC website, because rules are what govern you — not this article, and not any agent.

## How to check NMC approved colleges, step by step

Work through these steps in order, and write down what you find for each college you are considering.

**Step 1: Find the institution in the World Directory of Medical Schools.** Search the college's exact official name in the **World Directory of Medical Schools**, the global listing maintained with the World Federation for Medical Education and FAIMER. If the institution does not appear, treat that as a serious warning sign. Note the school's listing details and check that the programme named there matches the programme you are being offered.

**Step 2: Check the programme against every FMGL criterion.** Duration, English medium, clinical training and the same-institution internship must all hold for *your specific course*, not just for the university in general. Some universities run more than one programme, and a shorter or non-English track can fail the criteria even when another track passes. Get the course structure in writing.

**Step 3: Look for the Indian Embassy and student angle.** Indian embassies in several countries publish information or advisories about institutions attended by Indian students, and the experiences of current Indian students and recent graduates are a practical cross-check. Ask to speak to students who are actually studying there now — not only the ones an agent selects for you.

**Step 4: Verify the internship arrangement.** The FMGL framework requires a 12-month internship at the same foreign institution. Ask the college directly: is the internship part of the programme, is it in their own teaching hospitals, and will the completion documents state it clearly? Vague answers here are a red flag.

**Step 5: Cross-check claims against documents, never brochures.** Every claim that matters — duration, medium, internship, clinical postings — should be traceable to the university's own official documents or website. If a claim exists only in an agent's presentation, it does not exist.

## Red flags that should stop you immediately

Certain patterns appear again and again in problem cases. Walk away, or at minimum pause everything, if you see:

- **Pressure to pay quickly** — "seats closing today" is a sales tactic, not an academic deadline
- **A course shorter than 54 months** presented as somehow acceptable for India
- **Teaching partly in the local language** while the marketing says "fully English medium"
- **The college cannot be found** in the World Directory of Medical Schools
- **No clear answer on the internship** at the same institution
- **Guarantees about FMGE results** — no college or agent can guarantee an exam conducted by NBEMS in India
- **Reluctance to connect you with current students** studying at the college

None of these prove fraud on their own, but together they describe exactly the situations that later become eligibility disasters.

## Keep a verification file for every college

Treat this like the documentation habit you will need as a doctor. For each college, save the World Directory listing, the official course structure showing duration and medium, the internship details, and dated screenshots or PDFs of everything you relied on. If two colleges both pass the checks, this file lets you compare them on academics, clinical exposure and total cost rather than on marketing.

Also re-verify close to your admission date. Institutions change programmes, and regulations can be clarified or amended. The check you did in January should be refreshed before money moves in June. The authoritative sources are always the same: the NMC website for the FMGL Regulations, NBEMS for the FMGE, and the World Directory for the institution itself.

## After the college check: the FMGE is still yours to clear

A compliant college keeps you *eligible*; it does not make you a doctor in India. You will still face the FMGE — **300 MCQs, two parts of 150, no negative marking, pass mark 150** — and then the internship year in India. Q: What is the World Directory of Medical Schools and why does it matter?
A: It is the global listing of medical schools maintained with WFME and FAIMER. If a foreign institution does not appear in it, that is a serious warning sign, and you should verify its status very carefully before considering admission.
Q: Can an agent's assurance replace my own verification?
A: No. Only the regulations and the institution's official documents protect your eligibility. Check the World Directory listing, the course structure and the internship arrangement yourself, and keep written proof of everything you relied on.
Q: What happens after I return to India with a foreign degree?
A: You must clear the FMGE conducted by NBEMS (300 MCQs, pass mark 150, no negative marking) and then complete a 12-month CRMI internship in India before applying for permanent registration with your state medical council.
