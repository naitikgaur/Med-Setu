Preventive and Social Medicine looks vast until you notice how often the same programmes return in the paper. A large share of PSM questions on **national health programmes india fmge** aspirants face are not about obscure data. They ask which programme covers a disease, which agency runs it, what a newer name replaced, or what the stated objective of a scheme is. Those are learnable, repeatable marks. This guide organises the major programmes by name, nodal ministry or agency, and objective only.

## Why National Health Programmes India FMGE Questions Are a Goldmine

Most national programmes run under the **Ministry of Health and Family Welfare**, Government of India, and many now sit within the broader umbrella of the **National Health Mission**, which has rural and urban components. Questions reward recognition rather than calculation.

Examiners commonly test:

- The **full form** of an abbreviation such as NTEP, NACP, NVBDCP, or NPCDCS
- The programme that deals with a given disease or population group
- A **name change**, such as the Revised National Tuberculosis Control Programme becoming the National Tuberculosis Elimination Programme
- The nodal agency, such as **NACO** for HIV/AIDS or the Central TB Division for tuberculosis
- The main objective or components of a programme
- Which beneficiary group a maternal and child health scheme covers

Fix one line for each programme: its name, who runs it, and what it aims to do. Then test that recall in the MedSetu [question bank](/qbank/).

## Disease Control Programmes: TB, HIV, and Vector-Borne Diseases

**National Tuberculosis Elimination Programme (NTEP)**

- Earlier called the **Revised National Tuberculosis Control Programme (RNTCP)**, it was renamed NTEP to reflect the goal of eliminating tuberculosis as a public health problem in India.
- Implemented under the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare, with the **Central TB Division** as the nodal technical body.
- Its objective is early detection, complete and quality-assured treatment, and reduction of transmission, morbidity, and mortality from TB, including drug-resistant TB and TB associated with HIV.
- **Nikshay** is the web-based system associated with notification and tracking of TB patients.

**National AIDS Control Programme (NACP)**

- Implemented through the **National AIDS Control Organisation (NACO)** under the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare, with State AIDS Control Societies at state level.
- Its objective is to prevent new HIV infections, and to provide care, support, and treatment for people living with HIV.
- Its work includes targeted interventions for high-risk groups, behaviour-change communication, condom promotion, management of sexually transmitted infections, blood safety, counselling and testing, and antiretroviral treatment.

**National Vector Borne Disease Control Programme (NVBDCP)**

- This programme covers **vector-borne diseases**: malaria, dengue, chikungunya, Japanese encephalitis, kala-azar, and lymphatic filariasis, under the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare.
- Its objective is to prevent outbreaks and reduce morbidity and mortality through vector control, early case detection, and complete treatment.
- For malaria, remember the textbook sequence: the National Malaria Control Programme became the National Malaria Eradication Programme, and malaria activities now form part of NVBDCP.

## Universal Immunisation Programme and Child Health

The **Universal Immunisation Programme (UIP)** is one of the most tested programmes in PSM.

- Developed from the earlier Expanded Programme on Immunization, UIP provides free vaccination against vaccine-preventable diseases under the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare, as part of the National Health Mission.
- Its objective is to protect children, and pregnant women for tetanus-containing vaccination as per the schedule, against diseases in the **National Immunisation Schedule**, including tuberculosis, poliomyelitis, diphtheria, pertussis, tetanus, hepatitis B, measles and rubella, and other diseases added over time, including pneumococcal and rotavirus disease in the current schedule.
- **Mission Indradhanush** is the special drive associated with UIP, launched to reach children and pregnant women missed by routine immunisation.
- **Cold chain** maintenance is essential, because vaccine potency depends on correct storage and transport temperatures.

Child health also includes the **Rashtriya Bal Swasthya Karyakram (RBSK)**, which screens children for diseases, deficiencies, birth defects, and developmental delays, with referral for early intervention.

## Maternal Health Programmes: JSY, JSSK, and Related Schemes

Maternal schemes are tested by beneficiary and by what is provided, so learn them as a small family.

**Janani Suraksha Yojana (JSY)**

- JSY is a safe motherhood intervention under the **National Health Mission**, implemented by the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare.
- Its objective is to reduce maternal and neonatal mortality by promoting **institutional delivery** among pregnant women, particularly those from economically weaker households.
- It provides cash assistance linked to institutional delivery, and works through community health workers such as ASHAs, who are incentivised for facilitating institutional care.

**Janani Shishu Suraksha Karyakram (JSSK)**

- JSSK is also implemented under the National Health Mission by the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare.
- Its objective is to eliminate out-of-pocket expenditure for pregnant women and sick newborns using public health facilities.
- It provides free and cashless services in government facilities, including delivery and caesarean section, drugs and consumables, diagnostics, diet during the stay, and transport between home, facility, and referral facility, with entitlements for sick infants as per programme provisions.

The classic distinction is simple: **JSY promotes institutional delivery through cash assistance, while JSSK removes the costs of care and transport in public facilities.** Options frequently swap these objectives.

Also recognise **Pradhan Mantri Surakshit Matritva Abhiyan (PMSMA)**, which provides assured antenatal care by specialists on a fixed day each month; **LaQshya**, which aims to improve quality of care in labour rooms; and **Surakshit Matritva Aashwasan (SUMAN)**, which aims at dignified, quality healthcare at no cost for women and newborns in public facilities.

## NPCDCS, Mental Health, and Other Programmes Worth Knowing

**National Programme for Prevention and Control of Cancer, Diabetes, Cardiovascular Diseases and Stroke (NPCDCS)**

- Implemented under the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare within the National Health Mission framework.
- Its objective is to prevent and control common non-communicable diseases through health promotion, screening of adults, early diagnosis, and management and referral.
- Its focus conditions are in its name: cancer, diabetes, cardiovascular diseases, and stroke.

Other programmes to recognise by name and objective:

- **National Mental Health Programme (NMHP):** Aims to ensure accessible minimum mental healthcare for all and integrate mental health into general healthcare, with the District Mental Health Programme at district level.
- **National Programme for Control of Blindness and Visual Impairment:** Aims to reduce blindness and visual impairment through comprehensive eye care, including cataract surgery.
- **National Leprosy Eradication Programme (NLEP):** Aims to detect leprosy early and provide complete treatment to reduce transmission and prevent disabilities.
- **National Iodine Deficiency Disorders Control Programme:** Aims to control iodine deficiency disorders through iodised salt and monitoring.
- **National Tobacco Control Programme:** Aims to create awareness of tobacco harms, help people quit, and support tobacco control laws.
- **National Programme for Health Care of the Elderly:** Aims to provide accessible, comprehensive healthcare dedicated to elderly people.
- **RMNCH+A:** The Reproductive, Maternal, Newborn, Child and Adolescent Health approach under the National Health Mission organises services across life stages as a continuum of care.

## How to Revise Programmes Without Mixing Them Up

FMGE is conducted by NBEMS as 300 MCQs in two parts of 150 questions each, with no negative marking, and the pass mark is 150 out of 300. PSM is a scoring subject in that paper because so much of it, including programmes, is factual and revisable.

A method that works well is:

- Make a one-page table with three columns: programme, nodal agency, and one-line objective
- Group programmes by field — communicable diseases, non-communicable diseases, maternal health, child health
- Revise name changes separately, because RNTCP to NTEP and similar changes are asked directly
- Learn paired distinctions, such as JSY versus JSSK, as deliberate comparisons
- Attempt questions soon after revising, because agency-swapping distractors are only obvious once faced
