Six months is the most comfortable window for FMGE — long enough for a complete first reading, a full second pass, and proper mock-test training, without the panic of a crash plan. But six months only works if it is divided deliberately. This **fmge 6 month study plan** gives you a week-by-week structure built on the official subject weightage, so that by exam day you have not just "covered" the syllabus but revised it three times. The target throughout is simple: a safe score above the **pass mark of 150 out of 300**.

## The logic behind this FMGE 6 month study plan

The paper has **300 MCQs in two parts of 150**, **+1 per correct answer, no negative marking**, and is conducted by NBEMS twice a year. Subject weightage decides the order of study:

- **Medicine 33, Surgery 32, OBG 30, Community Medicine 30** — the big four, 125 questions
- **Anatomy, Physiology, Biochemistry 17 each; Paediatrics, Ophthalmology, ENT 15 each**
- **Pathology, Microbiology, Pharmacology 13 each; Forensic Medicine 10; Radiology 10**
- **Psychiatry, Dermatology, Anaesthesia, Orthopaedics 5 each**

The plan runs in four blocks: **Weeks 1–10** first reading of the big subjects, **Weeks 11–18** first reading of the remaining subjects plus weekly papers, **Weeks 19–22** second reading and grand tests, **Weeks 23–26** revision and mocks only.

## Weeks 1–10: the big four, one at a time

**Weeks 1–3 — Medicine.** The single largest subject. Work system by system: cardiology, respiratory, neurology, gastroenterology, nephrology, endocrinology, infectious diseases. Solve topic questions daily from the [question bank](/qbank/) as you finish each system — do not postpone questions to "later".

**Weeks 4–6 — Surgery.** General surgery, GI, trauma, burns, surgical oncology basics, then the allied topics. Keep a running list of classifications, staging systems and "most common" facts — Surgery questions love them.

**Weeks 7–8 — Obstetrics & Gynaecology.** Two weeks is enough if you are disciplined: labour, obstetric emergencies, antenatal care, contraception, common gynaecological conditions.

**Weeks 9–10 — Community Medicine.** Park-based, high-yield and very scorable: epidemiology, biostatistics basics, national programmes, vaccines, nutrition. Many candidates underrate SPM; the paper does not.

At the end of each subject, take the corresponding [subject-wise tests](/subjects/) and record your score. These numbers are your baseline — you will compare your grand-test performance against them later.

## Weeks 11–18: finish the syllabus, start full papers

**Weeks 11–12 — Anatomy, Physiology, Biochemistry.** The pre-clinical trio, 51 questions combined. Focus on high-yield areas: neuroanatomy, embryo developments that recur, cardiovascular and respiratory physiology, metabolic pathways and molecular biology basics.

**Weeks 13–14 — Pathology, Microbiology, Pharmacology.** The para-clinical trio, 39 questions. Robbins-based general pathology, organism–disease matching in microbiology, and drug mechanisms plus adverse effects in pharmacology.

**Weeks 15–16 — Paediatrics, Ophthalmology, ENT.** 45 questions. Milestones, immunisation, common childhood illness; eye anatomy, cataract, glaucoma, refractive errors; ear and throat conditions that appear again and again.

**Week 17 — Forensic Medicine and Radiology.** 20 questions from compact syllabi: legal procedures, poisons, injuries; imaging signs and investigation-of-choice questions.

**Week 18 — Psychiatry, Dermatology, Anaesthesia, Orthopaedics.** The four 5-mark subjects. Small syllabi, predictable questions — treat this week as collecting 20 cheap marks.

From **Week 11 onwards**, add one full paper every Sunday from the [previous-year papers](/papers/) — sat in one sitting, at exam time, then analysed the same evening. Classify each error: knowledge gap, silly mistake, or failed guess.

## Weeks 19–22: second reading plus grand tests

Now cycle back through everything, faster. A subject that took three weeks the first time should take three to four days now — you are revising notes and marked questions, not re-reading textbooks. Take two tests a week from the [grand test series](/tests/): one midweek, one on Sunday. After each test, use the analysis to find your weakest three topics and revise exactly those before the next test. This loop — test, analyse, patch, retest — is where scores actually climb.

## Weeks 23–26: revision and mocks only

No new material. Your weekly rhythm for the final month:

- **Monday–Friday:** revise one major subject per day from your notes and mistake list; solve 100 mixed questions daily in Exam mode.
- **Saturday:** full grand test under exam conditions.
- **Sunday morning:** analyse Saturday's test in detail. **Sunday evening:** light revision only, then stop early.

In the final week, taper: two light revision days, one last mock three days before the exam, and the day before kept for admit-card logistics, documents and sleep. Walk in having already sat the exam format a dozen times — the hall should feel familiar, not frightening.

## Weekly targets to keep you honest

- **First reading phase:** one subject block finished per schedule + 400–500 questions a week
- **Second phase:** syllabus completion + 1 previous-year paper a week
- **Second reading:** 2 grand tests a week, error log updated after each
- **Final month:** revision cycles only, mock score tracked every week

If your grand-test scores are consistently at or above 150 in the last month, your plan has done its job. If a week collapses — illness, family, life — do not abandon the plan; compress the current block and protect the final month. The last four weeks of revision and mocks are the part you must not sacrifice.

Start the plan today by checking where you stand: sit one full paper from the [previous-year papers](/papers/) this week, look at the eight-part analysis honestly, and let that baseline tell you how to weight your first ten weeks. Six months from now, that honesty is what 150-plus looks like.

Q: Is 6 months enough to prepare for FMGE?
A: Yes. Six months allows a complete first reading of all 19 subjects, a second revision pass, weekly previous-year papers and a final month of grand tests — provided you study consistently and protect the last month for revision.
Q: Which subjects should I study first in a 6-month FMGE plan?
A: Start with the highest-weightage subjects: Medicine (33 questions), Surgery (32), Obstetrics & Gynaecology (30) and Community Medicine (30). Together they are 125 of the 300 questions.
Q: How many mock tests should I take in 6 months of FMGE preparation?
A: A practical rhythm is one previous-year paper a week from the third month, rising to two grand tests a week in the second-reading phase, and one full mock every week in the final month.
Q: What is the pass mark I should target in mock tests?
A: The FMGE pass mark is 150 out of 300. In mocks, aim to be consistently at or above 150 in the final month so that exam-day variation does not put the result at risk.
