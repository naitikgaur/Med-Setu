Most exam-day anxiety is really uncertainty anxiety, and a clear **fmge exam day guide** removes most of it before you reach the centre. The FMGE is a **computer-based test** conducted by **NBEMS** at designated centres. The paper itself you know well by now: **300 MCQs in two parts — Part A with 150 questions, then a break, then Part B with 150 questions — +1 for every correct answer, no negative marking, pass mark 150 out of 300**. What students are less sure about is everything around the paper: what happens at the gate, what the security process involves, what the screen looks like, and how the two parts flow. This article walks through the standard process so that on the day, the only unfamiliar thing is the questions. Always confirm the specifics for your session — reporting time, permitted items, centre rules — from your admit card and the current NBEMS information bulletin, because those details are set per session and override any general guide.

## Your FMGE Exam Day Guide: Before You Leave — The Night and Morning

Exam day performance is built the day before. Keep the previous evening light: no new topics, no full mock, no group chats dissecting predictions. Lay out what the current bulletin and admit card require — your admit card and the specified identity documents are the essentials; check the current notice for the exact list rather than relying on memory or forwards. Plan the route to the centre with a generous margin. Centres and cities can be unfamiliar, and a rushed arrival spends the calm you saved by preparing well. Sleep properly. A rested candidate reads stems accurately; a sleepless one misreads them — and "EXCEPT" is the most expensive word in this exam to miss.

Eat a normal breakfast and carry water if the centre rules permit it. Arrive at the reporting time printed for you — not at the exam start time. The processes below take a while, and they are sequenced; late arrival risks being turned away under the session's rules.

## At the Centre: Reporting, Security and Biometrics

The standard flow at an FMGE centre follows the pattern of other national computer-based tests:

- **Entry and admit-card check.** Your admit card and identity are verified at entry. Keep them in hand, not in a bag you are about to surrender.
- **Frisking and security check.** Expect a security screening. Prohibited items — typically electronic devices and personal belongings beyond what is permitted — must be left outside the examination hall, so carry as little as possible.
- **Biometric registration.** Your photograph and fingerprint are captured and matched against your application record. This is routine and quick; it is also why your appearance should reasonably match your application photograph.
- **Seat allotment.** You are directed to a specific computer terminal. Your details appear on the screen; verify your name and photograph, and report any mismatch to the invigilator immediately, before the test begins.

Once seated, follow the invigilator's instructions about when to log in and begin. Rough sheets or their on-screen equivalent, where provided, are for working — use them for calculations and elimination notes.

## Inside the Test: The Screen and the Palette

The computer-based interface is deliberately simple, and it rewards candidates who have practised on a similar screen — one reason to rehearse with the [grand test series](/tests/) and [previous-year papers](/papers/) in exam mode before the day. The standard elements:

- **The question area** shows one question at a time with its four options. You select an answer by clicking an option.
- **The question palette** lists all 150 questions of the current part with colour or symbol codes showing their state — answered, unanswered, marked for review, or answered and marked. Learn these states in practice so you never waste a second decoding them in the hall.
- **Navigation controls** let you move to the next or previous question, jump via the palette, and mark a question for review.
- **The timer** counts down for the current part at the top of the screen. Glance at it at planned checkpoints — for example after every 50 questions — rather than watching it continuously.

A crucial structural fact: **the two parts are separate**. Part A must be completed and submitted within its sitting; you cannot carry time or questions across into Part B. When Part A ends, it ends — which is why pacing practice per part, not per paper, is the right training unit.

## The Break Between Parts

After Part A is submitted, there is a scheduled break before Part B begins. Use it as a break, not as a post-mortem. Do not reconstruct Part A questions with fellow candidates; nothing about Part A can now change, and a doubtful memory can poison a fresh part. Eat lightly if you have brought food as permitted, hydrate, move around, and reset. Part B is a new 150-question paper that happens to share your day — candidates who treat it that way consistently do better than candidates who carry Part A's emotions into it.

## A Working Rhythm for Each Part

Within each part, the rhythm that suits this format — and that you should have rehearsed — is a two-pass flow. First pass: answer the confident questions, mark the resistant ones, keep moving. Second pass: return to marked questions, eliminate, decide, attempt. Final minutes: confirm that **nothing is unattempted** — with no negative marking, a blank is the only answer that is certainly wrong. Submit when your review is done or time expires; there is no prize for finishing early, and unused minutes are better spent checking marked questions than sitting idle.

If a technical problem occurs — a frozen screen, a system restart — raise your hand immediately and stay calm. Invigilators have standard procedures for this, and your responses are recorded on the system. Do not attempt to fix anything yourself beyond following instructions.

## After the Exam

Results are declared by NBEMS after the session, through its official channels. Until then, resist reconstructing your score from memory and recalls; memory of 300 questions is unreliable and the exercise only produces anxiety. Whatever the outcome, the process after passing involves the next official steps toward internship and registration — follow the current NMC and NBEMS notices for those, in order.

Exam day rewards the prepared twice: once in knowledge, once in calm. Rehearse the format until the screen feels familiar — a timed paper on MedSetu's [previous-year papers](/papers/) page is the closest practice you can get — and let the day itself hold no surprises except the questions.
